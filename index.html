The model

Ten rules. Nothing here needs technology the state does not already run.

Every rupee collected is visible

Tax receipts post to a single public ledger. Anyone can watch the balance accumulate in real time.

Approval debits the source

When the Union sanctions money to a state, it leaves the national account and lands in the state's account as one identified transaction.

States release to districts

The same rule applies one tier down. A state cannot spend what it has not received, and cannot release what it has not recorded.

Districts release to talukas

And talukas to individual works. The chain is the same shape at every tier, so one mechanism covers the whole country.

…and so on, to the last mile

The chain ends at a work order against a named project with a named engineer responsible for it.

The receiver must acknowledge

No transfer is complete on the sender's word alone. The receiving office signs for what actually arrived.

Agreement turns the line green

Sender's figure and receiver's figure reconcile — the public sees a green path and no one has to do anything further.

Spending is itemised in public

Infrastructure, equipment, imports, research — each bill names the vendor and the amount, and the vendor acknowledges receipt too.

A gap raises a red flag

Deviation beyond the permitted margin auto-generates a show-cause notice to the department that received the money.

Justify, or face action

An accepted explanation closes the case and the line turns green. An unaccepted one triggers recovery, suspension or prosecution — and that outcome is public too.

Why the 1% margin exists

Real transfers lose small amounts to bank charges, rounding, exchange rates and mid-year tax revisions. A zero-tolerance rule would drown the system in false alarms and destroy its credibility in a week. A narrow published margin absorbs honest noise while leaving deliberate diversion nowhere to hide — diversion is never 0.9%.

Move the Permitted deviation slider at the top of the page and every status on this site recomputes live. At 0% almost everything is flagged; by about 1% only genuine gaps remain. That is the argument for the number, made visible.

What already exists

The state already runs PFMS for fund flow, the GSTN for invoice-level reconciliation, Aadhaar-seeded DBT for last-mile transfers, and TIN for individual tax records. Each is a working tier of this chain. What is missing is not capability — it is a single public surface that joins them and an obligation to acknowledge.

Honest limits of this demo

Four districts per state and two blocks per district are traced; the untraced remainder is carried as an explicit aggregate node so every tier still balances to the rupee. Acknowledgement timings, discrepancy rates and enforcement outcomes are modelled, not measured. Ratings are stored in your browser only. Nothing here is a claim about any real official, department or vendor.
